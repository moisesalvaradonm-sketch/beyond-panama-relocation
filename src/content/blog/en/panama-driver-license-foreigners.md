A foreign tourist license in Panama is valid for 90 days from your entry date — not 90 days per trip, and not renewable by leaving and re-entering the country the way some old forums still claim. Police checkpoints now routinely check entry stamps against the date on your license. Get this wrong and you're driving unlicensed, which voids your insurance in a crash.

That single fact trips up more new arrivals than any other part of driving here. If you have legal residency in Panama, you need a Panamanian license. Here is how to get one.

## Who needs a Panamanian driver's license?

The rules differ depending on your immigration status:

- **Tourists:** Your foreign license is valid for 90 days from entry. No Panamanian license needed for short visits.
- **Temporary residents:** Once residency is approved and you have a provisional ID (carnet provisional), you should begin the license process. Most immigration attorneys recommend starting within the first 60 days.
- **Permanent residents:** Required to obtain a Panamanian license. Driving on a foreign license after permanent residence is approved is technically illegal.

## The ATTT: Panama's traffic authority

The **Autoridad del Tránsito y Transporte Terrestre (ATTT)** is the government agency responsible for driver's licenses, vehicle registration, and traffic enforcement in Panama. All license transactions go through them.

ATTT has offices in Panama City (the main one is in San Miguelito district), David (for Chiriquí province), and regional offices in other provinces. The ATTT website exists but its information is not always current — go directly to the office or ask your immigration attorney for the current process.

## The license exchange (canje): the fastest route

If your home country has a **reciprocal agreement** with Panama, you can exchange your existing foreign license for a Panamanian one without taking a written test or driving exam. This is called the *canje* (exchange), and it is significantly simpler than starting from scratch.

Countries with reciprocal agreements include: **United States, Canada, most European Union countries, United Kingdom, Australia, Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, Brazil, Chile, and others**. The list is managed by the ATTT and can be updated by regulation.

If your country is on the reciprocal list, the canje process applies to you.

### Documents required for the license exchange

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| Valid foreign driver's license (original) | Must be current, not expired |
| Panamanian residence card | Provisional or permanent |
| Passport (original + copy of photo page) | |
| Passport copy showing entry stamp | |
| Medical fitness certificate | Issued by an ATTT-approved clinic in Panama |
| Blood type certificate | Usually done at the same clinic |
| Cedula (if you have it) | Or your carnet provisional |
| Copy of your foreign license | Front and back |
| Completed ATTT application form | Provided at the office |
| Payment | Government fees |

The medical certificate and blood type certificate can be obtained at clinics located near the ATTT offices. The exam is brief: vision check, blood pressure, and basic coordination. Cost is typically $20–$35 USD at private clinics near the ATTT.

### License categories in Panama

| Category | Vehicle type |
|---|---|
| B | Passenger vehicles (cars, SUVs, pickups) — the standard category |
| C | Vehicles over 3.5 metric tons (trucks, buses) |
| A | Motorcycles |

Most expats apply for Category B. If you drive a standard car or SUV, this is what you need.

## Step-by-step process

**Step 1: Get your medical certificate**
Visit an ATTT-authorized medical clinic near the ATTT office. The exam takes 15–20 minutes. You'll receive a certificate with your vision result, blood type, and a medical clearance. Cost: $20–35.

**Step 2: Assemble your document package**
Gather all required originals and copies from the list above. Make at least two copies of everything. The ATTT accepts original-plus-copy for most documents.

**Step 3: Go to the ATTT office early**
ATTT offices are busy. Arrive before 8:00 AM or expect a significant wait. The main Panama City office at San Miguelito opens at 7:30 AM. Bring everything — they will send you away for a missing document.

**Step 4: Submit the application**
A staff member will review your documents. If everything is in order, they will process the application on the spot. You may need to wait 1–3 hours depending on office volume.

**Step 5: Pay the fees**
Government fees for a Category B license exchange run approximately $40–$60. There may be additional small fees for the application form or administrative processing.

**Step 6: Pick up your license**
In most cases, the license is not issued the same day. A card will be ready for pickup in 2–4 weeks, or sometimes mailed to an address on file. Ask the ATTT officer directly about the pickup timeline at your specific office.

## Timeline

From start to finish, allow **2 to 4 weeks** from the day you submit your application to the day you receive your license. The actual ATTT processing is often just a few business days; the delay is usually in the physical card production and distribution.

## If your country doesn't have a reciprocal agreement

If your country of origin is not on Panama's reciprocal agreement list, the canje is not available. You will need to obtain a Panamanian license through the standard process, which includes:

1. Enrolling in a licensed **driving school** (*academia de conducción*) in Panama
2. Completing a mandatory training course (typically 16–20 hours of theory + practical)
3. Passing a written exam (in Spanish) at the ATTT
4. Passing a practical driving exam

Cost at driving schools: **$150–$300**, depending on the school and the number of practice hours included. The written exam is conducted in Spanish; some schools offer study materials in English, but the official exam is Spanish-only.

## Renewal and validity

A standard Panamanian driver's license (Category B) is valid for **4 years**. Renewal requires a new medical certificate and payment of renewal fees, but no re-examination.

## Common pitfalls

**Waiting too long after residency approval.** The 90-day tourist license window does not restart when you get residency. If you've already used your tourist period and are now a resident, you're in a grey zone. Get your license application started immediately after residency approval.

**Showing up with an expired foreign license.** Your foreign license must be valid at the time of the canje. An expired license cannot be exchanged. If yours has expired, you'll need to renew it in your home country first (some countries offer renewal by mail or online for overseas residents) or go through the driving school route.

**Using unofficial translators.** If any of your foreign documents need translation, Panama requires translations by a certified translator recognized by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. A translation done by a private bilingual person is not accepted.

**Missing copies.** The ATTT requires copies, not just originals. Bring a full set of photocopies of every document in your package before you arrive.

**Driving during the process period.** Once your foreign license passes its 90-day mark and you are a resident, you are technically unlicensed until your Panamanian license arrives. Avoid driving during this period, or arrange to have someone else drive while you wait.

## Practical notes

Your Panamanian license becomes your primary driving identification from the moment it is issued. It is also accepted as a form of ID in many everyday situations in Panama.

If you're in Panama City, the San Miguelito ATTT office handles the majority of expat license transactions. Some private immigration services offer to manage the ATTT process for you — this can reduce the time you spend in queues, at a cost.

Your Panamanian license will also be needed when registering a vehicle in your name, updating your car insurance policy, and setting up certain financial accounts.
