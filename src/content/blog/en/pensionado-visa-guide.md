The **Panama Pensionado Visa** is, by a wide margin, the most sought-after retirement residence in Latin America. It's not by accident: the requirements are reasonable, the benefits are real, and the process — while bureaucratic — is predictable.

This guide explains who qualifies, what you need, and what no one tells you about the actual process.

## Who can apply for the Pensionado Visa?

The main requirement is having a **permanent lifetime pension from any source** of at least **$1,000 USD per month**. This includes:

- Social Security pensions from any country (U.S. Social Security, U.K. State Pension, etc.)
- Government or military pensions
- Private corporate pensions from established employers
- Permanent disability pensions

The key word is **lifetime**: the pension must be permanent, not savings or retirement funds that can be depleted. A 401(k) or savings account doesn't qualify directly, but a lifetime annuity does.

**Important note:** $1,000 is the minimum for the primary applicant. If you include a dependent in the application (spouse or other), the requirement increases by $250 per additional dependent.

## Pensionado Visa benefits

The benefits aren't marketing — they're established in Panamanian law (Law 6 of 1987 and its amendments):

- **50% discount** on national entertainment (cinemas, parks, cultural events)
- **30% discount** on public transportation (buses, trains)
- **25% discount** on restaurant meals
- **15% discount** on health service bills
- **20% discount** on professional medical services
- **Import tax exemption** on household goods (up to a certain value, applicable once)
- **Income tax exemption** on foreign-source income

The restaurant and healthcare discounts are the ones most commonly used in daily life.

## Required documents

The typical documents requested (may vary depending on your legal advisor):

1. **Pension letter** from the issuing body, apostilled and translated into Spanish
2. **Valid passport** with at least 6 months of validity
3. **Criminal background check** from your home country, apostilled
4. **Health certificate** from a Panamanian doctor (local medical exam)
5. **Passport-size photos**
6. **Life insurance policy** with coverage in Panama

All foreign documentation must be **apostilled** in the country of origin and **translated by a certified translator** if not in Spanish.

## How long does the process take?

The full process, from document collection to approval and ID card issuance, typically takes **3 to 6 months**. The factors that extend the timeline most:

- Delays in apostilling documents in your home country (each country has its own timelines)
- Corrections or additional documents requested by Immigration
- Workload at Panama's Immigration offices

There is no official fast track. The only way to speed things up is to get the documents right on the first attempt.

## What no one tells you

**The process is done locally.** You can't initiate it from your home country. You must be physically in Panama to submit the application, complete the medical exam, and — when approval comes through — pick up your ID card. Many applicants combine this with an exploratory visit.

**Work with an immigration attorney based in Panama.** Not because the process is impossible without one, but because document errors restart timelines. A local professional knows what documents Immigration is actually accepting at that moment (practice can differ from what the law says on paper) and can follow up on your file.

**The pension doesn't have to be from the U.S.** Although the Pensionado Visa is especially popular with North Americans, it applies to pensions from any country. Retired Europeans, Latin Americans, and people from other regions also qualify if they meet the amount and permanence requirements.

**Residency is permanent from the start.** Unlike other visas that are temporary and convert to permanent after a period, the Pensionado Visa grants permanent residency directly.
